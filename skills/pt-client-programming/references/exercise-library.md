# Exercise library (seed)

Side-neutral descriptions in the clinician's voice. "The working arm" / "the target leg" become "your right arm" / "your left leg" when adapted for a client. Add new entries only with the clinician's description or approval. Copied to `library/exercises.md` at setup; edit that copy.

Format: `### Name` · region tags · description paragraph · optional `Progression:` paragraph · optional typical dose (a starting point for DISCUSS, never an automatic dose in BUILD).

## Shoulder — mobility and control

### Wall slide
Stand facing a wall with the forearms resting on it, elbows about shoulder-width apart. Slide the forearms upward along the wall only as far as the shoulders stay comfortable and relaxed, then lower slowly. Keep the ribs quiet and avoid shrugging toward the ears. Regress by sliding on the pinky side of the hands or using less height.
Progression: Add a light band around the hands and keep gentle outward tension while sliding.

### Supine shoulder flexion
Lie on your back with the working arm by your side and your thumb pointing upward. Lift the arm forward toward the ceiling and then toward overhead, only as far as comfortable; lower slowly along the same path. Keep your ribs quiet and avoid arching your back or shrugging toward your ear. Stop short of a pinch rather than trying to stretch through it.
Progression: Once unweighted motion is smooth, add a very light weight, such as 0.5–1 lb, and return to 6–8 repetitions.

### Supported shoulder rotation (internal and external, supine)
Lie on your back with the working upper arm about 45 degrees away from your side and your elbow bent to 90 degrees. Support the elbow and upper arm on a folded towel or small pillow so the elbow sits slightly forward of your shoulder; begin with your forearm pointing toward the ceiling. Keep the elbow still as you slowly tip the forearm toward your feet for inward rotation and toward your head for outward rotation. Keep the front of the shoulder gently back against the surface without forcefully pinning it down; stop before it rolls forward, pinches, or your back arches.
Progression: Move the elbow gradually toward 90 degrees away from your side, or reduce the prop height. Change one at a time. Add a very light weight last.

### Side-lying shoulder external rotation
Lie on the non-working side with your head supported and the working elbow bent to 90 degrees. Place a small folded towel between the working upper arm and your ribs, with the forearm resting across your abdomen. Keep the elbow at your side as you rotate the forearm upward, then lower slowly. Keep your trunk still; avoid rolling backward, lifting the elbow, or shrugging.
Progression: Add 0.5–1 lb only when 12 unweighted repetitions are easy and controlled.

### External-rotation isotonic (band)
Stand with the working elbow bent to 90 degrees and tucked at your side, holding a light band anchored across your body. Rotate the forearm outward against the band through a comfortable range, then return slowly. Keep the elbow at your side and the shoulder relaxed. Build repetitions before resistance; reduce range or band tension if yellow.

### Internal-rotation isotonic (band)
Stand with the working elbow bent to 90 degrees and tucked at your side, holding a light band anchored to the working side. Rotate the forearm inward across your body through a comfortable range, then return slowly. Keep the elbow at your side and the front of the shoulder relaxed. Build repetitions before resistance; reduce range or band tension if yellow.

### Isometric shoulder abduction at wall
Stand with the working side beside a wall, feet steady, working arm near your side, and elbow bent. Place a folded towel between the outside of your upper arm just above the elbow and the wall. Gently press outward into the wall as if lifting your arm sideways, but keep the arm and body still. Breathe normally and keep your neck relaxed; feel gentle effort at the side of the shoulder without leaning into the wall or pushing through a pinch.
Progression: Increase hold time before pressure. Move to isotonic abduction with a light weight once holds are green.

### Shoulder abduction isometric to isotonic
Begin with the wall isometric above, 5 holds of 10–20 seconds. When holds are green, lift the arm sideways through a comfortable range with no weight, building comfortable range before adding weight. Reduce pressure, time, or arm angle if yellow.

## Shoulder — strength and weight bearing

### Scapular push-up plus and weight shift
Place your hands on a wall or a high surface at shoulder height, arms straight. Keeping the elbows straight, let the chest sink slightly between the shoulder blades, then press the upper back toward the ceiling. Add small side-to-side weight shifts between the hands. Keep the neck long and the ribs quiet. Progress from the wall to a lower incline, then to a shallow push-up; regress the height or range.

### Supine fly with light weights
Lie on your back with light weights in both hands, arms toward the ceiling, elbows softly bent. Open the arms outward only as far as the shoulders stay comfortable, then bring them back together with control. Keep the ribs down and the back relaxed. Add repetitions, then weight, then opening range; reduce weight or range if yellow.

### Reverse fly
Hinge forward at the hips with a flat back, or lie face down on a bench for chest support, light weights in hand. Lift the arms out to the side toward a T position, squeezing between the shoulder blades, then lower slowly. Keep the neck long and avoid shrugging. Add repetitions, weight, then arm angle toward a T; add chest support or bend the elbows to ease it.

### Standing band row
Stand facing the anchor with a band in both hands, arms forward. Pull the hands toward the ribs, drawing the shoulder blades back and down, then return slowly. Keep the trunk tall and the neck relaxed. Progress from low elbows to higher elbows as assigned; reduce resistance or angle if yellow.

### FlexBar rhythmic stabilisation
Hold the bar in both hands in front of you, elbows bent, and create a gentle oscillation through the bar while keeping the shoulders steady. Begin lying on your back or with bent elbows; progress to longer arms forward, then farther to the side. Reduce time, speed, resistance, or arm length if yellow.

### High-anchor pulldown
Hold a band anchored above head height with the working arm, beginning below full overhead reach. Pull down toward the hip with the elbow leading, then return slowly. Build comfortable reach, then repetitions and resistance.

### Overhead pulldown
Hold a band or cable anchored overhead with both hands through the cleared overhead range. Pull toward the upper chest, drawing the shoulder blades down, then return slowly. Reduce reach or resistance if needed.

### Feet-supported active hang
Hold a secure bar with the feet on a stable platform carrying most of your weight. Draw the shoulders gently down away from the ears and hold, breathing normally. Build holds toward 15–20 seconds, then use slightly less leg support. Do not drop into a hang.

### Assisted pull-up
Use feet on a stable platform or an assisted machine. Pull smoothly through the cleared range and lower with control. Build smooth range and repetitions before reducing assistance.

### Unloaded reach behind the hip
Stand tall and reach the working hand a small, comfortable distance behind the hip with the elbow straight or softly bent. Increase reach without arching the back or rolling the shoulder forward.

### Light-band shoulder extension
Hold a band anchored in front of you with the working arm. Pull from slightly in front of the hip to the hip, then a little behind when green. Keep the shoulder back and the ribs quiet. Build range before band tension.

### Supported extension weight shift
Place the hands on a secure surface slightly behind the hips with the feet bearing most of your weight. Shift a small amount of weight into the hands and return. Add a small shift before reducing leg support.

### Assisted dip support and shallow dip
Use secure parallel handles and strong foot or machine assistance. Start in the top support position, then add a small lower-and-press motion within a controlled depth. Return to supported shifts if yellow.

## Hip — mobility

### All-fours hip hinge
Begin on your hands and knees with your hands under your shoulders and knees under your hips. Keep a small, comfortable arch in your low back as you shift your hips toward your heels. Keep your pelvis level and avoid rounding or twisting through your back. Stop before you feel a firm pinch in the hip.

### Hip-flexion mobilisation (banded)
Place the target foot on a low step or chair and loop a band high around the thigh, with the band pulling from behind you. Keep the foot planted and the knee pointing in the same direction as your toes. Slowly shift forward to bring the knee farther over the foot without twisting your pelvis. Use a comfortable range and back off if you feel a hard pinch in the front of the hip.

### Hip-abduction mobilisation (banded)
Loop a band high around the target thigh and anchor it out to that side. Step into a wide stance and shift into a small side lunge over the target leg. Keep the foot planted, knee aligned over the foot, and belt line level. Use a comfortable range without forcing a groin pinch or allowing the knee to collapse inward.

### Hip internal-rotation mobilisation (banded)
Begin on your hands and knees with a light band looped high around the target thigh and anchored out to that side or slightly behind you. Keep the target knee steady and slide the foot outward slightly to create a small amount of hip rotation. Maintain that position as you gently shift your hips toward your heels. Keep your pelvis quiet and reduce the range if you feel knee pain or a hard groin pinch.

## Hip and trunk — control

### Chair squat
Stand in front of a chair with your feet in a comfortable position. Reach your hips back and lower to the chair with control, keeping your knees aligned over your feet. Gradually place more weight through the target leg as you sit and stand. Avoid twisting, shifting sharply to one side, or pushing away from the target side.

### Bridge with weight shift / march
Lie on your back with your knees bent and feet planted. Press through both feet and lift your hips while keeping the front of your pelvis level. Gently shift your weight toward the target side without arching your low back or allowing the opposite side of your pelvis to drop. Return to the centre with control before lowering.
Progression: Progress toward a gentle march by lifting the non-target foot a small distance.

### Side-lying hip external rotation
Lie on the non-target side with the target leg on top, knees bent, and feet together. Stack your pelvis so the top hip stays directly over the bottom hip, then gently open the top knee while keeping your feet together. Keep the top hip slightly forward and stop before your pelvis or low back rolls backward. You should feel the muscles along the outside or back of the hip working rather than your low back.

### Side-lying hip internal rotation
Lie on the non-target side with both knees bent and place a pillow between your knees. Keep light pressure on the pillow as you lift the top foot upward, allowing the thigh to rotate inward. Keep your pelvis stacked and avoid rolling forward, rolling backward, or moving through your low back. Use a small, smooth range without forcing the motion.

### Standing march balance
Stand tall near a counter or another stable support. Slowly lift one knee while keeping your pelvis level and your stance leg steady. Keep your lower trunk controlled while allowing your upper body and breathing to remain relaxed. Use light fingertip support or reduce the height of the march if you begin leaning or hiking the hip.

### Standing hip abduction
Stand tall while holding a counter for balance. Keep your toes pointing forward as you move one leg a small distance out to the side. Keep your belt line level and your trunk upright without side-bending or hiking the hip. Use a smaller range if your pelvis begins to move or your toes turn outward.

### Resisted side stepping
Hold the band with both hands at the centre of your chest and stand perpendicular to the anchor so the band pulls from one side. Begin with your feet about hip-width apart, knees slightly bent, and enough band tension to challenge you without pulling you off balance. Take controlled sideways steps away from the anchor, then return without allowing your feet to come completely together. Keep your feet pointing forward, pelvis level, and trunk upright without leaning toward or away from the anchor. Turn around between sets so the band pulls from the opposite side.

### Hip leveler
Stand beside a low surface with the hand and same-side knee supported on it. Keep the spine steady while lifting and lowering the free-side hip a small distance. Keep the supporting side long and the spine quiet.

### Bird dog — opposite arm and leg
Begin on your hands and knees, with your hands under your shoulders and knees under your hips. Reach one arm forward and the opposite leg backward, then return with control. Keep your pelvis level and your trunk quiet; reach long without arching your back or rotating your hips. Shorten the reach if you cannot maintain that position.
Progression: Add a light band around the hands or feet. Add the opposite arm only when the base version is green.

### Airplane
Balance on one leg, hinge forward at the hips with hips and shoulders facing the ground, then return to a tall march. Keep the stance knee and toes tracking forward.

### Wall press hip hinge
Stand on one leg beside a wall and press the free knee into the wall. Keep full-foot contact and a comfortable low-back arch while hinging forward at the hips, then return.

### Side plank dip — bottom knee bent
Lie on your side with your elbow under your shoulder, bottom knee bent, and top leg straight. Press through your forearm and bottom knee to lift your hips, then slowly lower and lift your pelvis through a controlled range. Keep your shoulders and hips stacked, with your pelvis in line with your trunk; do not let the top shoulder roll forward or the top hip roll backward. Make the dip smaller if you lose this alignment.

### Running side plank
From the side plank position with the bottom knee bent, lift the hips, drive the top knee forward, then return with control. Avoid arching or twisting.

### Dead bug
Lie on your back with the knees over the hips and the arms toward the ceiling. Keep the low back gently steady as you slowly lower one leg, or opposite arm and leg, toward the floor, then return. Keep breathing and stop before the back loses control.

### 90/90 hold — feet on wall
Lie on your back with the feet on a wall, hips and knees at 90 degrees. Lightly tilt the pelvis so the low back gently flattens. Relax the hips and breathe.

### Heel slides
Lie on your back with the knees bent. Slide one heel away along the floor and return. Keep the pelvis quiet and avoid a painful pop.

### Foot hover marches
Lie on your back with the knees bent. Float one foot 1–2 inches off the floor, then lower slowly. Do not yank from the hip.

### Tabletop hold and alternating tabletop
Lie on your back and lift one leg to tabletop (hip and knee at 90 degrees); hold without shifting, then lower slowly. Progress to alternating legs slowly, reaching the foot to a higher surface if needed to keep the motion quiet.

### Toe taps (elevated, then full)
From tabletop, tap one foot to a block or chair, then return with smooth control. Progress to lowering the foot toward the floor for 3–4 seconds and returning in about 2 seconds.

## Lower body — loading and carrying

### Forward step-up
Face a low, stable step with one foot fully supported on it and a rail or counter nearby. Press through the foot on the step to bring yourself up, then lower slowly. Keep your pelvis level and knee aligned over your foot; avoid hiking the hip, leaning sideways, or pushing strongly from the trailing foot. Use a lower step or light hand support if needed.

### Side step-up
Stand beside a low, stable step and place the nearer foot fully on it. Press through that leg to lift yourself onto the step, then lower slowly back to the starting position. Keep your pelvis level, trunk upright, and knee aligned over your foot without hiking the hip or leaning sideways. Use a lower step or light hand support if needed.

### Suitcase carry
Hold a dumbbell or kettlebell in one hand by your side and stand tall. Walk with slow, even steps while keeping your shoulders and pelvis level. Avoid leaning toward or away from the weight, hiking a hip, or holding your breath. Repeat with the weight in the other hand; use a lighter weight or shorter walk if your posture or walking changes.

### Lunge to march with row
Step into a shallow lunge holding a band anchored in front. Row the band toward the ribs as you rise into a steady march on the front leg. Keep the trunk tall and the pelvis level.

### Tabletop hip extension
Lie face down across a firm counter or table with the legs hanging. Lift one leg without increasing the low-back arch; first set slow, later sets may lift more quickly.

### Bridge march
Lie on your back with the knees bent and feet planted. Lift the hips, then alternate lifting one foot a small distance while keeping the pelvis level. If this increases back symptoms, use tabletop hip extension instead.

## Calf and heel

### Two-leg floor heel raise
Hold a stable support. Rise through both forefeet and lower slowly to the floor, keeping pressure through the big-toe side without rolling out. No heel drop below the forefoot at this step.

### Single-leg floor heel raise
Use hand support for balance. Rise on the target leg and lower slowly to floor level. Use both feet to rise and the target leg to lower if needed.

### Weighted floor heel raise
Add a weight belt or other resistance selected with {{clinician_first_name}}. Use two legs or one according to control, rising and lowering slowly. Keep the heel at floor level at the bottom.

### Bent-knee heel raise
Use a supported standing heel raise with a small knee bend maintained throughout, or a seated raise with resistance over the thigh. Avoid sinking farther forward at the bottom.

### Single-leg bent-knee heel raise
Stand on the target leg with a small knee bend. Rise, pause, and lower slowly. Keep the knee from drifting inward.

### Gentle dorsiflexion
With support and the heel down, slowly move the knee forward over the foot, then return. Use a small comfortable range without forcing a stretch at the back of the heel.

### Shallow heel drop
With both forefeet on a secure step and hand support, raise, then lower just below forefoot level within the range assigned. Begin with a small drop and no added weight.

## Band press and row family

### Pallof press / forward press
Stand perpendicular to the band's anchor point with your feet approximately shoulder-width apart. Hold the band with both hands at the centre of your chest. Keep your trunk upright and facing straight ahead. Press your hands forward without allowing the band to rotate your body, then slowly return your hands to your chest.
Progression: Add a small, controlled trunk rotation as you press forward. Return to a neutral trunk position as your hands come back toward your chest. Complete the exercise with the band pulling from both directions.

### Overhead press (band)
Stand perpendicular to the band's anchor point with your feet approximately shoulder-width apart. Hold the band with both hands at chest height. Keep your trunk upright and facing straight ahead. Press the band overhead while resisting the band's pull. Avoid arching your lower back or allowing your trunk to rotate, then slowly return your hands to the starting position.
Progression: Add a small, controlled side bend toward the band's anchor point and then away from it. Return to an upright, neutral position between movements. Complete the exercise with the band pulling from both directions.

### Staggered-stance unilateral press
Secure the band behind you and hold it in one hand. Stand with one foot slightly in front of the other. Begin with your trunk upright and in a neutral position. Press the band forward with one arm while keeping your trunk stable, then slowly return to the starting position. Complete the exercise on both sides.
Progression: Add a small, controlled trunk rotation in the direction of the press. Keep your pelvis stable and your feet firmly planted. Return your trunk to neutral before beginning the next repetition.

### Staggered-stance unilateral row
Secure the band in front of you and hold it in one hand. Stand with one foot slightly in front of the other. Begin with your trunk upright and in a neutral position. Pull the band toward you with one arm while keeping your trunk stable, then slowly return to the starting position. Complete the exercise on both sides.
Progression: Add a small, controlled trunk rotation in the direction of the row. Keep your pelvis stable and your feet firmly planted. Return your trunk to neutral before beginning the next repetition.

## Spine and neck — daily reset

### Mountain valley (cat / cow)
On hands and knees, slowly round from tailbone to head, then return from head to tailbone. Use a small range if extension feels sensitive.

### Leg sway
Lie on your back with knees bent. Let the legs move side to side, then the hips, then the low back. Return upright from the top of the spine down.

### Tail wag
On hands and knees, bring the hip and shoulder on the same side gently toward each other. Keep the movement relaxed and smooth.

### Wall ball chin nod
Face the wall with a ball at the forehead. Keep the chest close to the wall and the head stacked over the shoulders. Press gently and nod "yes" without clenching the jaw.

### Nerve sliders
Move the knee, ankle, and head as taught. This is a glide, not a held stretch. Stop if tingling or symptoms move farther down the limb.

### Pelvic tilt
Lie on your back with knees bent. Gently flatten the low back, then create a small comfortable hollow. Stay within the motion that feels best.

## Self-care tools

### Ball massage against the wall
Place a ball between the wall and the muscles of the outer or back hip (or the area {{clinician_first_name}} showed you). Lean gently and move slowly over the muscle, 30–60 seconds per area. Avoid bony points and any area that produces sharp or spreading symptoms.

### Abdominal massage for constipation (routine)
Use the 8–10-minute routine from the language blocks: settle (1 min), warm-up circles (1 min), follow the colon path clockwise (3 min), I–L–U strokes (2 min), comfort circles (1.5 min), finish (1.5 min). Pressure 2–3/10, no digging. Do not massage with constant or severe belly pain, vomiting, fever, blood, or a hard swollen belly; contact a medical professional for those.
