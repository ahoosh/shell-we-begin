# Your goal

Build comfortable movement and strength for reaching into cupboards and lifting at home. We will use four exercises, starting with supported movement and light effort, then adding load as your shoulder tolerates it.

Your findings suggest a sensitive rotator cuff, the group of muscles that help guide and lift your arm. These are working impressions, not confirmed diagnoses.

# Your symptom guide

| Zone | What you notice | Your next move |
|------|-----------------|----------------|
| **GREEN** | Movement is comfortable or only mildly uncomfortable, stays controlled, and feels confident. Symptoms settle quickly, and you are no worse later that day or the next morning. | Continue or gradually progress. |
| **YELLOW** | Symptoms increase more than expected, you shrug or lose control, or soreness lasts into the next day. | Reduce range, effort, repetitions, sets, or frequency. Try the easier version and reassess. |
| **RED** | Sharp pain, significant catching, a painful pop, sudden loss of strength or motion, or symptoms that keep increasing or do not settle after reducing the exercise. | Stop and contact {{clinician_first_name}}. Sudden severe pain, inability to move the arm, or persistent new numbness warrants prompt medical assessment. |

# Adjusting challenge and recovery

Your body adapts to the right challenge over time, and recovery is part of that process. Exercise is only one part of your load. Carrying, chores, work, travel, and recreation also count. Busy days may need a lighter shoulder session.

A yellow day does not mean failure. Use a smaller range, lighter effort, fewer repetitions, or an easier version of these same exercises. This can be an active recovery day. Build again when movement, confidence, and your next-day response return to green.

Change one thing at a time: comfortable range and control first, then repetitions, then resistance and daily-task demands. Stay with each change for several green sessions. If your reaching range decreases, return to the unweighted supine flexion. If rotation decreases, return to supported rotation at about 45 degrees.

# Your weekly plan

These are provisional starting ranges because we have not yet established your home-exercise tolerance or recovery pattern. Start at the lower end while we learn your response.

| Category | Days per week | How to use it |
|----------|---------------|---------------|
| Mobility and control | 4–6 | Unweighted supine flexion and supported internal/external rotation. Keep sessions short and easy. |
| Strength | 2–3 | Side-lying external rotation and wall abduction holds. Later, weighted flexion and rotation belong here too. Leave at least one recovery day between strength sessions. |
| Active recovery | 1–3 as needed | Use a smaller, easier dose of the same unweighted mobility/control exercises. This replaces a harder session. |

**Scheduling notes:** Categories can share a day. Use the lower end with yellow symptoms, fatigue, poor sleep, or heavier daily activity. Use the upper end only with stable green responses and good recovery. Do not double up after a missed day.

# Phase 1 — Comfortable movement and control

Begin without hand weights. These starting doses are guides, not targets to push through. Rest about 30–60 seconds between sets or longer if needed. Full directions are in the Exercise menu.

| Exercise | Starting dose | Progression or regression |
|----------|---------------|---------------------------|
| Supine shoulder flexion | 1 set of 6–8 slow repetitions | Build comfortable range, then toward 10–12 repetitions. Reduce the arc or bend the elbow slightly if needed. |
| Side-lying external rotation | 1 set of 6–8 slow repetitions; no weight | Build toward 10–12 controlled repetitions. Use a smaller outward arc or fewer repetitions if sore. |
| Supported internal/external rotation | 1 set of 5–8 cycles in and out; no weight | Start near 45° away from your side with the elbow propped forward. Increase only the range you can control. |
| Isometric abduction at wall | 4 holds of 5–10 seconds; rest 15–20 seconds | Use gentle effort, about 20–30% of your maximum. Ease the pressure or shorten the hold if sore. |

**Pinching or neck symptoms:** Do not push through a pinch at the top or front of the shoulder. Shorten the motion or lighten the effort. Keep your head supported; stop if an exercise brings on head pain, spreading arm symptoms, or a marked increase in neck pain.

# Phase 2 — Build strength and reaching tolerance

Move into this phase when the starting exercises feel controlled and you have several green sessions in a row, including the next morning. Each exercise can progress at its own pace. Keep easier movement as a warm-up or recovery option; you do not need every version at every session.

| Exercise | Next dose | Progression or regression |
|----------|-----------|---------------------------|
| Supine shoulder flexion | Build to 2 sets of 8–12 | Once unweighted motion is smooth, add a very light weight, such as 0.5–1 lb. Return to 6–8 repetitions. Remove weight or shorten the arc if yellow. |
| Side-lying external rotation | Build to 2 sets of 8–12 | Add 0.5–1 lb only when 12 unweighted repetitions are easy and controlled. Reduce repetitions first; remove weight if you roll or shrug. |
| Supported internal/external rotation | Build to 1–2 sets of 8–12 cycles | Move the elbow gradually toward 90° away from your side, or reduce prop height. Change one at a time. Add a very light weight last. |
| Isometric abduction at wall | Build toward 4–5 holds of 15–20 seconds | Increase hold time before pressure. Keep effort moderate and smooth. Use lighter pressure or shorter holds if symptoms rise. |

**Rotation checkpoint:** Only advance if the front of your shoulder stays relaxed and does not roll forward during inward rotation. Reaching 90° or removing the prop is not a deadline. Keep the supported position if it feels better.

# Adjust daily activities while symptoms settle

**Carrying:** Keep loads close to your waist with your elbow bent. Use both hands for heavier items and divide groceries into smaller loads.

**Reaching:** Step closer before reaching. Keep frequently used items below shoulder height. Shorten chores into manageable bouts.

**Testing:** Avoid forcing your arm behind your back or repeatedly testing a painful arc. A pinch is a cue to change the angle, range, or load; it does not by itself tell us that damage is occurring.

# Return to everyday reaching and lifting

Start with light items close to your body below shoulder height. After several green exposures, increase either the number of lifts, the reach distance, or the load, not all three. Add higher reaching with light items before heavier overhead work. Check your response during the task, later that day, and the next morning; if yellow, return to the last comfortable level.

# Exercise menu

Use these directions for your right shoulder. Set up on a firm bed or exercise surface that is easy to get on and off. Use a pillow that keeps your neck comfortable.

| Exercise | How to do it |
|----------|--------------|
| Supine shoulder flexion | Lie on your back with your right arm by your side and your thumb pointing upward. Lift the arm forward toward the ceiling and then toward overhead, only as far as comfortable; lower slowly along the same path. Keep your ribs quiet and avoid arching your back or shrugging toward your ear. Stop short of a pinch rather than trying to stretch through it. |
| Side-lying external rotation | Lie on your left side with your head supported and your right elbow bent to 90 degrees. Place a small folded towel between your right upper arm and ribs, with your forearm resting across your abdomen. Keep the elbow at your side as you rotate your forearm upward, then lower slowly. Keep your trunk still; avoid rolling backward, lifting the elbow, or shrugging. |
| Supported internal/external rotation | Lie on your back with your right upper arm about 45 degrees away from your side and your elbow bent to 90 degrees. Support the elbow and upper arm on a folded towel or small pillow so the elbow sits slightly forward of your shoulder; begin with your forearm pointing toward the ceiling. Keep the elbow still as you slowly tip the forearm toward your feet for inward rotation and toward your head for outward rotation. Keep the front of the shoulder gently back against the surface without forcefully pinning it down; stop before it rolls forward, pinches, or your back arches. |
| Isometric abduction at wall | Stand with your right side beside a wall, feet steady, right arm near your side, and elbow bent. Place a folded towel between the outside of your upper arm just above the elbow and the wall. Gently press outward into the wall as if lifting your arm sideways, but keep the arm and body still. Breathe normally and keep your neck relaxed; feel gentle effort at the side of the shoulder without leaning into the wall or pushing through a pinch. |
