# Style guide

The clinician's voice, distilled from their handouts. Match it; don't improve on it.

## Voice

- **Second person, present tense, warm.** "Your body adapts to the right challenge, and recovery is part of the process."
- **Plain words first, term second.** "Shoulder extension means moving your arm behind your body." "Isotonic means moving against resistance." Explain once, then use the term.
- **Working impressions, never diagnoses.** "Your findings suggest a sensitive rotator cuff, the muscles that help guide and lift your arm… These are working impressions, not confirmed diagnoses." Imaging is context, not the plan: "your symptoms and function will guide the program rather than the scan or calendar alone."
- **Permission to back off, without shame.** "A yellow day means changing the dose, not failure." "No make-up workouts. A modified or missed day does not create a debt."
- **Sentences are short.** One instruction per sentence. Lists of adjustable variables are fine in one sentence: "Reduce range, resistance, repetitions, sets, speed, or time."
- **Noise and sensation are information.** "A painless click with steady control may remain green." "A painless pop that does not change symptoms is information, not a challenge to repeat."
- **The client owns the decision.** "Allow the athlete to own the range, speed, and traffic-light choice."

## Words to use / avoid

| Use | Avoid |
|-----|-------|
| working impression, findings suggest | diagnosis, you have, tear, damage |
| yellow day, change the dose | flare-up (except in the flare plan), setback, failure |
| comfortable range, controlled | pain-free, full range, perfect form |
| contact {{clinician_first_name}} or another healthcare provider | see a doctor, go to the ER (unless RED criteria truly warrant "prompt medical assessment") |
| the working arm / the target leg (in library text) | left/right (until adapted for the client) |
| build toward, progress one small variable | push through, max out, as tolerated |

## Dose grammar

| Form | Example |
|------|---------|
| Sets × reps | `2 sets of 8–12` |
| Holds | `4 holds of 5–10 seconds; rest 15–20 seconds` |
| Rounds / bouts | `2–3 rounds of 15–30 seconds`, `1–2 bouts` |
| Per side | `2 sets of 8–12 each side`, or `2 × 8–10/side` in athlete-style programs |
| Steps | `2 sets of 8–12 steps with each hand` |
| Time | `20–40 min`, `6–10 minutes, 1–2 times per day` |
| Frequency | `2–3 days per week`, `4–7` in the weekly table |
| Effort | `about 20–30% of your maximum`, `3–5/10 effort` |
| Angles | `about 45 degrees` or `45°`; pick one per document |
| Load | `0.5–1 lb`, `a light band` |
| Intervals | `30 sec jog / 90 sec walk × 6–8` |

Rules:
- En dash (–) for ranges, never a hyphen. `×` for multiplication in athlete-style tables.
- "Start with" dose first, "build toward" dose second: "Start with 1 set of 6–8, then 1–2 sets of 8–12."
- Progress order, always: comfortable range and control → repetitions → sets → resistance → complexity → speed or activity-specific demand. "Change one variable at a time."
- Rest: "Rest 60–90 seconds between sets or holds, longer if needed. Finish before fatigue changes control."

## Progression / regression column

Each cell: one progression sentence, then one regression sentence. Keep both concrete.

> Add a light band around the hands. Regress to a pinky-side slide or less height.

> Build toward 2 sets of 12–15 with steady height. Regress to a smaller range, more hand support, or fewer repetitions.

If the clinician reserves a progression for themselves, say so: "{{clinician_first_name}} will reassess daily-activity tolerance before the loaded steps."

## How to write an exercise description (Exercise menu)

Four to six sentences in this order:

1. **Set-up position.** "Lie on your back with the working arm by your side and your thumb pointing upward."
2. **The movement.** "Lift the arm forward toward the ceiling and then toward overhead, only as far as comfortable; lower slowly along the same path."
3. **What stays still.** "Keep your ribs quiet and avoid arching your back or shrugging toward your ear."
4. **What to feel / what not to push through.** "Stop short of a pinch rather than trying to stretch through it."
5. (Optional) **Make it easier.** "Use light fingertip support or reduce the height of the march if you begin leaning."
6. (Optional) **Progression:** as a second paragraph starting with `Progression:`.

Name exercises consistently across the phase table and the menu: same words, same capitalisation. Side-specific wording is applied when the library entry is adapted for the client ("your right arm", "lie on your left side").

## Headings

- Heading 1 for every main section. Use sentence case except the words GREEN / YELLOW / RED.
- Phase headings: `Phase 1 — Comfortable motion and basic control` (em dash, or a plain space if the clinician's older documents use that).
- Complex-pattern programs number the sections: `1. Daily planning & symptom check`, `2. …`.
- Heading 2 inside a section for routines (`Routine A — …`), sub-plans (`The return path`), and warm-ups.

## Bold labels

Paragraph-level notes under a table begin with a bold label and a colon: `**Stop signals:**`, `**Progression:**`, `**Scheduling:**`, `**Rotation checkpoint:**`, `**Coach note:**`. Keep these; they are how the clinician signals "read this even if you skip the rest."

## Markers (never in approved files)

- `[[CONFIRM: starting dose for standing band row]]` – the clinician must supply or confirm.
- `[[MISSING: image for supine flexion]]` – a referenced asset isn't there.
- `[[SUGGESTION: …]]` – only in DISCUSS output or visit notes, never in a draft handout.

## Things the clinician decides, not you

Exercise selection · doses · which ladder step today · what needs reassessment before loading · return-to-activity stage · any precaution · appointment dates · anything about other clinicians, coaches, or family members.

You may propose any of these in DISCUSS, clearly labelled. In BUILD and PATCH they are inputs, not outputs.
